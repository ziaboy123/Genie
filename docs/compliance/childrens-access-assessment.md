## Part 1: children's access assessment

### Stage 1: can children get to Genie?

Yes. The Terms say account holders must be 13 or older, but there's no age check of any kind, and share pages open for anyone with the link. Children can only be treated as unable to use a service if it uses highly effective age assurance, and Genie doesn't.

### Stage 2: the child user condition

The question is whether a significant number of children use Genie, or whether it's the kind of service likely to attract them. Ofcom says "significant" can be a small number, and is judged against the size of the service.

- Birthday and Christmas wishlists are something teenagers (13 to 17) make, and the Terms allow them to.
- Lists go to family, so children of any age will open a parent's, sibling's or cousin's list and may grant a wish.
- The design is deliberately for all ages, and there's nothing on the service aimed only at adults.

**Conclusion: Genie is likely to be accessed by children.** The children's risk assessment (Part 2) and the duties to protect children (s.12) apply.

## Part 2: children's risk assessment

### What children can meet on Genie

Only lists someone has sent them: list and wish names, the owner's name, prices, and the domains of the links. No pictures, no comments or messages, and no way to find other lists. A child can't be contacted through Genie, and nobody can see who granted a wish.

### Age groups

Account holders can be 13 to 17 (and adults). Visitors can be any age, from young children opening a family list to teenagers. The risk is the same across Ofcom's age bands (0 to 5, 6 to 9, 10 to 12, 13 to 15, 16 to 17): every child only sees lists sent to them, and nothing on Genie is shown to one age group and not another.

### Content harmful to children

| Kind (OSA ss.61 and 62) | How it could reach a child on Genie | Likelihood | Impact | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pornography (primary priority) | A sexual list or wish name, or a link to a porn site, on a list sent to a child. No images are hosted, and the Terms ban sexual content and links | Low | High | Low |
| Suicide, self-harm, eating disorders (primary priority) | Text in a name, or a link. The Terms ban it | Negligible | Severe | Negligible |
| Abuse and hate (priority) | Hateful names on a list a child opens | Low | Medium | Low |
| Bullying (priority) | A list made to mock a child, passed round a class group chat. The most realistic risk to children here | Low | Medium | Low |
| Violence, harmful substances, dangerous stunts (priority) | Links to that kind of content. The Terms ban content encouraging them | Negligible | Medium | Negligible |
| Non-designated content (anything else harmful to children) | None identified | | | None |

### Risks to teenagers who make lists (not about content)

- **Their name and wishes travel further than meant** if a link is forwarded. Measures: the sign-up form says a first name is enough; links are unguessable and hidden from search engines; a new link kills the old one.
- **Being contacted by strangers.** There's no route for this on Genie: no messages, profiles or comments, and grants are anonymous.
- **Scam links sent to a teen.** Covered by the illegal content measures (real domain shown on every wish, reporting, removal).

### Measures

- Terms ban every kind of primary priority content for everyone: sexual or pornographic content and links, and content that encourages, promotes or gives instructions for suicide, self-harm or eating disorders (s.12(5)). They also ban hate, harassment, bullying, and content encouraging serious violence, dangerous stunts or harmful substances, and apply to visitors as well as owners.
- The Terms' "Keeping children safe" section explains how children are protected (harmful content banned for everyone, reported content removed, no search or messages) and how the 13+ rule works: no age check, you confirm your age at sign-up, and under-13s' accounts are deleted (s.12(9) and (11)).
- "Report this list" on every share page, with the email address shown next to it, read by a person; removal script; complaints by email (see handling-reports.md).
- Under-18s are told to check with a parent or guardian (sign-up page, Terms and Privacy).
- No search, discovery, recommendations, feeds or contact features, which are the features that usually push harmful content to children.

**Age checks:** none planned. Ofcom's Protection of Children Codes aim highly effective age assurance at services that allow, or are at medium or high risk of, the most harmful kinds of content. Genie bans every kind of primary priority content for all users (s.12(5)), and the risk is low. Check this against the current Codes at each review, and revisit straight away if reports show sexual content appearing.

**Overall: low risk to children.**
